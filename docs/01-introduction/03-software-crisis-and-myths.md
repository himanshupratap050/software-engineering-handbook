## 1. Exam-Ready Definition
**Software crisis** is the situation, recognised in the 1960s, where software projects were routinely late, over budget, unreliable and hard to maintain, because hardware capability and demand for software outgrew the ability to build it systematically.

## 2. Core Concept
The term became widely known around the **1968 NATO conference** on software engineering. As computers became powerful, software grew larger and more complex, but development was still ad hoc.

**Symptoms**
- Projects exceed time and budget.
- Software does not meet user requirements.
- Poor quality and many defects.
- Software is hard to maintain and modify.
- Poor or missing documentation.

**Causes**
- Growing size and complexity.
- Poor planning and estimation.
- Lack of systematic methods and tools.
- Changing and unclear requirements.
- Poor communication between users and developers.
- Insufficient training and inadequate testing.

**Remedy:** adopt software engineering: defined processes, methods, tools, quality assurance and project management.

**Software myths** are widely believed but false statements that mislead people.

| Group | Myth | Reality |
|---|---|---|
| Management | "We already have a book of standards, so the team will follow it." | Standards must be used, adapted and kept up to date. |
| Management | "If we are behind schedule, add more programmers." | Adding people to a late project can make it later (Brooks' law), because of training and communication overhead. |
| Customer | "A general statement of objectives is enough to start." | Vague requirements are a main cause of failure. |
| Customer | "Requirements can change easily because software is flexible." | Late changes are costly; the cost grows as the project advances. |
| Practitioner | "Once the program works, the job is done." | Most effort comes *after* delivery (maintenance). |
| Practitioner | "Quality can be assessed only after the program runs." | Reviews can assess quality early. |
| Practitioner | "The only deliverable is the working program." | Documentation and other work products are also deliverables. |

## 5. Key Points for Revision
- Crisis = late, over budget, poor quality software (1960s onwards).
- Causes: complexity, poor planning, no methods, changing requirements.
- Remedy: software engineering.
- Three myth groups: management, customer, practitioner.
- Brooks' law: adding manpower to a late project makes it later.

## 9. Common Exam Mistakes and Confusing Pairs
- Mixing **causes** and **symptoms**.
- Giving a myth without its **reality**.
- Placing a myth under the wrong group (management, customer, practitioner).
